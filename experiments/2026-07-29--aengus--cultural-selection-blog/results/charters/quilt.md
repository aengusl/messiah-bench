We make whole things out of scraps, and we do it together. Every block is cut from cloth that was already something else: a worn shirt, a flour sack, a dress outgrown. Set them in rows, repeat the pattern until the eye trusts it, and let the warm calicos argue gently at the seams. The stitching shows. We want it to show, small dashes marching over every join, because the join is the work. One pair of hands cannot finish a quilt. That is the lesson.
